Reading and rebuilding this report

This report retains the analyses and model specifications in results.qmd, separates confirmatory tests from robustness checks and exploratory analyses, and completes the trial-level confidence-resolution and continuous-collectivism analyses. The local preregistration is PSA-JTF3 EC Manuscript.pdf.

Render with quarto render results_v2.qmd --to html (or --to docx / --to pdf). The first render fits uncached models. Subsequent renders reuse intermediate objects and output in results_v2_cache/knitr-* and fitted models in results_v2_cache/models. Input-file checksums, R/package versions, and upstream analysis code invalidate chunk caches; model caches additionally track the fitting call and its actual data. Editing narrative text does not refit models. To force recomputation of all active analyses, remove only results_v2_cache before rendering. Existing source files and saved models are read only.

The existing measurement-invariance objects and the original individual-difference models are retained as legacy fitted results: their provenance cannot be verified from the saved objects alone. The original, disabled refitting code is retained for reference; those results are not silently presented as newly fitted models. Written interpretations inherited from the original report should be checked against the tables after any data or model change.

Analysis notes. The original code includes CAPTCHA scores equal to 0.5 (>= 0.5), whereas the preregistration excludes them. That cutoff is retained here for comparability. The exclusion-table total is corrected to include the residence exclusion. The confidence-resolution models retain the working specification with fixed problem effects and participant random intercepts; the preregistration instead specifies random intercepts for both participant and problem. The revised individual-difference models retain the original omission of AOT. The calibration models retain the original treatment of missing/excluded trials when summing accuracy.

As an exploratory test of cross-country measurement invariance, we fitted multi-group CFAs in which the nine items loaded on a substantive intellectual-humility (IH) factor while separate positive- and negative-wording method factors captured the method effects identified in the original scale validation. The IH factor was specified as independent of the wording factors, while the two method factors were allowed to covary. The configural model showed good fit (CFI = .973, RMSEA = .050, SRMR = .029). We then constrained only the substantive IH loadings to equality across countries, leaving wording-factor loadings free; this produced a modest deterioration in fit (ΔCFI = −.017, ΔRMSEA = .005, ΔSRMR = .017). Adding equality constraints on item intercepts produced a larger deterioration (ΔCFI = −.032, ΔRMSEA = .009, ΔSRMR = .016). Thus, using the conventional ΔCFI ≤ .01 criterion, strict metric and scalar invariance were not supported, although departures from metric invariance were relatively modest. These results suggest that the scale captures a broadly similar construct across countries, but some caution is warranted.

1.4.6.1 Binned observations for the calibration plots

The additional plot versions overlay participant-level mean estimates of performance, with ±1 standard error bars. Only participants with complete data for the relevant phase and IH contribute to a bin. Accuracy levels 1–6 are displayed, giving 12 median-split points or 30 quintile points per phase; zero-correct observations remain in the fitted models and original plots.

For the accuracy-axis plots, one median IH cutoff is calculated across participants in the calibration dataset and used for both phases and every accuracy level. Scores equal to the median belong to the lower group, so ties may make the group sizes unequal. Point shapes distinguish these two observed groups; the original three curves still show predictions at mean IH and ±1 SD, rather than predictions averaged over the median-split groups.

For the IH-axis plots, participants are sorted by IH within each phase and accuracy level and divided into five bins with counts differing by at most one. Ties in IH are ordered by participant ID and may span bins. Points are placed at each bin’s mean IH and mean estimated performance, and their colours match the corresponding accuracy curves. Error bars describe variability across participant estimates; they are distinct from the model confidence bands.
